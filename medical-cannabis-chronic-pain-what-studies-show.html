Medical Cannabis for Chronic Pain: What Two Big 2026 Studies Found

8 min read · November 2026 · By Anne M.

If you live with chronic pain, you probably know the routine. Pain relievers take the edge off for a few hours and physical therapy helps on good weeks, but some days still disappear to an aching back or burning nerves.

You're far from alone. About 1 in 5 US adults lived with chronic pain in 2019, according to CDC figures cited in a review of medical cannabis for chronic pain. It's also the most common reason people sign up for medical cannabis.

Two large studies published in 2026 followed thousands of these patients in everyday life to see what changed after they started. In Minnesota, almost 55% of patients with moderate-to-severe pain said it interfered less with their day, and they enjoyed life more, within 4 months. In Canada, patients who used cannabis under a doctor's care reported less pain, better mood and a better quality of life over 6 months.

At a Glance

  • Almost 55% of Minnesota patients who started with moderate-to-severe scores improved in life enjoyment and daily activity within 4 months.
  • Most patients improved no matter which type of cannabis product they chose, and 78% chose flower.
  • Canadian patients under a doctor's care improved in pain, anxiety, depression and quality of life over 24 weeks, with modest changes on average.

What the Minnesota Medical Cannabis Study Found About Chronic Pain

Minnesota is one of the few states that tracks how medical cannabis patients feel over time. Before each purchase, patients rate their pain and how much it gets in the way of their life. That gives researchers a running record from thousands of real people.

For the Minnesota study published in Clinical Therapeutics, the state's Office of Cannabis Management looked at more than 6,000 patients certified for chronic or intractable pain. They all enrolled between March 2022 and February 2023 and kept buying medical cannabis for at least 8 months. Their scores were checked when they started, at 4 months and at 8 months.

Almost 55% of patients who started with moderate-to-severe scores improved in life enjoyment and daily activity within 4 months. For someone who has spent years planning each day around pain, that can mean getting back to a walk with friends or a full shift at work.

Patients chose their own products, and 78% went with botanical cannabis, the dried flower. Most of them improved no matter which product type they used. For you, that means you may have more than one option to try with your doctor.

How Medical Cannabis Improved Daily Life for Chronic Pain Patients

The Minnesota patients rated themselves on the PEG scale, a quick three-question check that doctors use for chronic pain. It asks how bad your pain is, how much it gets in the way of enjoying life and how much it disrupts your general activity, such as work, chores and getting out of the house.

Splitting pain into those three parts showed something useful. A 2026 analysis of the Minnesota study found that improvements in life enjoyment and daily activity showed up within 4 months for many patients, while pain intensity improved less often. In everyday terms, some people still felt pain but found it took up less of their day.

An earlier state report pointed the same way. It tracked 9,961 patients with pain-related conditions, and 31.7% of those with moderate-to-severe pain had a meaningful drop in pain within 4 months. Almost half of them, 49.7%, kept that improvement for another 4 months, according to the Minnesota Office of Cannabis Management chronic pain report.

The report also found that many patients who were taking prescription pain medicines when they enrolled were using less of them at 6 months. If you take daily pain relievers, ask your doctor about this before changing any dose.

In Summary

This study suggests that if chronic pain is getting in the way of your work, sleep or social life, medical cannabis may help you get more of your day back, even when the pain itself only eases a little. Many people noticed a difference within 4 months, whichever product they chose.

What the Canadian Study Found About Doctor-Supervised Medical Cannabis

The second study came from Canada, where medical cannabis is often authorized and followed up by a health care provider. Dr. Hance Clarke, director of pain services at Toronto's University Health Network, led the research. It was published in the Canadian Journal of Pain in January 2026.

The team followed adults with chronic pain across several Canadian provinces for 24 weeks. Patients chose their products with guidance from a physician and filled in standard health questionnaires at the start, then at 6, 12 and 24 weeks.

By week 24, patients had improved on every measure the team tracked, including pain, anxiety, depression and quality of life. On a 0 to 10 pain scale, the average score dropped by about 1.2 points. Anxiety and depression scores also came down.

The average changes were modest. None reached the level researchers call a "minimal clinically important difference," the smallest change most patients would notice day to day. Averages can hide a wide range, though, and the authors say clinician follow-up helps show which patients are benefiting.

In Summary

This study suggests that medical cannabis, used with a doctor's guidance, may ease chronic pain a little and lift your mood and quality of life over 6 months. It works best as part of your wider pain treatment, with a doctor who can check how it's working for you.

Why Doctor Supervision Helps With Medical Cannabis Pain Relief

Both studies followed patients who used cannabis through a medical program, and the Canadian team made doctor supervision central to its research. Dr. Clarke's group says clinicians play an important part in checking how well cannabis is working, how safe it is for each patient and how they're doing over time.

A big reason is choice. Medical cannabis products vary widely in their mix of THC and CBD, their strength and how you take them, from flower and oils to capsules and edibles. A 2026 review of doctor-recommended cannabis use in California notes that people with complex health conditions may especially benefit from a clinician's help with that choice.

A doctor or cannabis-trained clinician can help you pick a product and starting dose to suit your type of pain. They can also check how it fits with medicines you already take and adjust the plan at follow-up visits. That's how the Canadian patients were cared for, with regular check-ins at 6, 12 and 24 weeks.

THC vs CBD for Pain and Questions to Ask Your Doctor About Medical Cannabis

THC and CBD are the two best-known compounds in cannabis. THC causes the "high," and CBD doesn't. For pain, products that contain THC have the most research behind them.

A 2026 systematic review of cannabis-based products for chronic pain in the Annals of Internal Medicine pooled 25 short-term studies with more than 2,300 patients. Products with higher levels of THC, alone or balanced with CBD, may give small improvements in pain, while products that were mostly CBD had little effect on pain. THC products also brought more dizziness, sleepiness and nausea, which is why getting the dose right with your doctor helps.

In the Minnesota study, most people improved whichever product they chose. That's useful to know if your first product doesn't suit you, since another one might.

These questions can help you get the most from your first appointment.

  • Which type of product and THC-to-CBD balance suits my kind of pain?
  • What starting dose do you suggest, and how slowly should I increase it?
  • Does cannabis interact with any of my current medicines, including pain relievers or sleep aids?
  • How will we track if it's working? You can rate your own pain, enjoyment of life and daily activity each week, just like the Minnesota patients did.
  • When is it safe for me to drive or work after a dose?
  • When should we review the plan together?

In Summary

Research so far suggests that products containing some THC are more likely to help with pain than CBD-only products. Starting low with your doctor's guidance helps you find relief with fewer side effects.

Where the Evidence on Medical Cannabis for Chronic Pain Stands Today

The Minnesota and Canadian studies are real-world studies. They followed people in their everyday lives instead of comparing them with a placebo group, so they show what happened to patients but can't prove cannabis alone caused every improvement. They're still valuable, because they show how medical cannabis works for thousands of ordinary patients outside a lab.

Placebo-controlled trials come next, and some are already running. Researchers in Colorado are testing medical cannabis for chronic pain, which is where the evidence looks most promising, according to UCHealth's report on Colorado cannabis trials.

US research may also speed up. In April 2026, the Department of Justice moved state-licensed medical cannabis and FDA-approved cannabis products from Schedule I to Schedule III. Schedule III comes with lighter federal registration rules than Schedule I, which could make it easier for scientists to study medical cannabis, as explained in this legal overview of cannabis rescheduling.

In Summary

Today's studies give real reasons for hope, especially for people whose pain disrupts their daily lives. Bigger controlled trials are under way, and easier research rules should bring clearer answers on who benefits most and which products work best.

Key Takeaways

  • Two large 2026 studies found that many people with chronic pain felt better after starting medical cannabis, especially in daily activity, enjoyment of life and mood.
  • Almost 55% of Minnesota patients with moderate-to-severe scores improved within 4 months.
  • Products containing some THC have the most research behind them for pain.
  • Working with a doctor helps you choose a product, start at the right dose and track your progress.

Frequently Asked Questions About Medical Cannabis for Chronic Pain

Does Medical Cannabis Help Chronic Pain?

For many people, yes. In a Minnesota study of more than 6,000 patients, almost 55% of those with moderate-to-severe scores enjoyed life more and found pain got in the way less within 4 months. A Canadian study also found improvements in pain, mood and quality of life over 24 weeks.

How Long Does Medical Cannabis Take to Work for Chronic Pain?

Most patients in the Minnesota program noticed a change within their first 4 months. In the Canadian study, patients checked in at 6, 12 and 24 weeks, and they had improved by week 24.

Is Cannabis Flower or Oil Better for Chronic Pain?

There's no single best form. In the Minnesota study, 78% of patients chose flower, but most improved whichever product they used. Your doctor can help you pick a form and strength that suit your pain and your routine.

Can Medical Cannabis Replace Opioids for Chronic Pain?

A Minnesota report found that many patients who were taking prescription pain medicines when they enrolled were using less of them at 6 months. Never stop or cut down an opioid on your own, though. Your doctor can plan any change safely with you.

Do I Need a Doctor to Use Medical Cannabis for Pain?

In most places with a medical program, yes. A health care practitioner has to certify or authorize you. Both 2026 studies followed patients in medical programs, and the Canadian team said ongoing clinician follow-up was an important part of care.
